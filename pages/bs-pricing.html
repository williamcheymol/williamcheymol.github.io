PHASE 0 COMPLETE C++ QUANTITATIVE FINANCE

Black-Scholes
PDE Pricer

A C++ solver for the Black-Scholes PDE using two independent finite-difference schemes, with full Greeks computation and benchmark against closed-form analytical formulas.

THE PROBLEM

The fair value of a European option does not just pop out of thin air — it satisfies a partial differential equation. Under the Black-Scholes model, any derivative $V(t, S)$ written on an asset following Geometric Brownian Motion obeys the BS PDE, a parabolic equation in $(S, t)$ space with a terminal condition at maturity.

Solving this PDE numerically gives option prices and Greeks on the entire $(S, t)$ grid in a single backwards sweep — far more efficient than running a Monte Carlo simulation for each individual spot price.

This project implements two distinct finite-difference schemes — one in native $S$-space, one in log-price space — and validates both against the exact Black-Scholes formulas for price and all five Greeks.

BLACK-SCHOLES PDE
$$\frac{\partial V}{\partial t} + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 S^2 \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial S^2} + rS\frac{\partial V}{\partial S} - rV = 0$$
Solved backwards in time on a discrete $(S, t)$ grid, from the terminal condition $V(T, S) = \text{Payoff}(S)$ down to $t = 0$.
NUMERICAL METHODS
METHOD 01 — Full equation via Crank-Nicholson Scheme

Uniform S-grid

Standard centred finite differences on $S_i = i \cdot \Delta S$ discretise the spatial operator $\mathcal{L}[V]_i = a_i V_{i-1} + b_i V_i + c_i V_{i+1}$, with variable coefficients that grow with $S_i$.

The Crank-Nicolson scheme averages the implicit and explicit levels at each time step, producing a tridiagonal system solved in $O(N)$ via the Thomas algorithm.

Accuracy O(Δt², ΔS²)
Stability Unconditionally stable
Grid Uniform in S
METHOD 02 — Reduced Equation via Finite Difference Scheme

Log-price space

The substitution $x = \ln S$ transforms the BS PDE into a constant-coefficient equation. The diffusion term loses its $S^2$ growth, and all grid nodes share the same tridiagonal stencil.

The log-uniform grid gives finer relative resolution near the strike, where the option is most sensitive. After solving in $x$-space, $V(x_i)$ is interpolated back to the uniform $S$-grid for output.

Accuracy O(Δt², Δx²)
Stability Unconditionally stable
Grid Log-uniform in S
GREEKS

All five Greeks are computed from the solved PDE grid. Delta, Gamma and Theta are recovered for free from spatial finite differences and the PDE identity — no extra solver calls needed. Vega and Rho use bump-and-reprice, each requiring two additional solves.

GREEK DEFINITION METHOD COST
Delta $\Delta$ $\partial V / \partial S$ Central FD on solved grid Free
Gamma $\Gamma$ $\partial^2 V / \partial S^2$ Central FD on solved grid Free
Theta $\Theta$ $\partial V / \partial t$ PDE identity: $\Theta = -\mathcal{L}[V]$ Free
Vega $\nu$ $\partial V / \partial \sigma$ Bump-and-reprice $\sigma \pm \varepsilon$ 2 solver calls
Rho $\rho$ $\partial V / \partial r$ Bump-and-reprice $r \pm \varepsilon$ 2 solver calls

Theta is recovered directly from the PDE: since $\partial V / \partial t = -\mathcal{L}[V]$, Theta is simply the spatial operator evaluated at the solution — more accurate than finite-differencing in time and requires no extra storage.

SAMPLE OUTPUT
