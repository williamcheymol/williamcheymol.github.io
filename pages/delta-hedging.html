Delta & Gamma
Hedging Simulation

A Python simulation of European option hedging under Black-Scholes — from GBM path generation to P&L attribution, extended with a live implied volatility surface built from real SPY option chains.

THE PROBLEM

When a trader sells an option, they collect a premium but take on directional risk: the option's value moves with the underlying. Delta hedging neutralises this exposure by holding $\Delta$ shares per option sold, making the portfolio locally immune to small price moves.

In theory, continuous rebalancing under Black-Scholes replicates the option perfectly — the P&L is zero on every path. In practice, hedging is discrete (daily, weekly), which introduces a residual tracking error that shrinks as rebalancing becomes more frequent.

This project simulates both delta hedging and delta-gamma hedging across up to 1,000,000 Monte Carlo paths, quantifying the error, studying the effect of rebalancing frequency, decomposing the P&L into gamma and theta contributions — and comparing flat-vol results against a live implied volatility surface.

Simulate paths

BLACK-SCHOLES FRAMEWORK

Under Black-Scholes, the stock price follows a Geometric Brownian Motion: $$dS = \mu S \, dt + \sigma S \, dW$$ Under the risk-neutral measure ($\mu \to r$), the fair value of a European call is given in closed form. The model assumes constant volatility, no dividends, and frictionless markets.

The simulation is run under the risk-neutral measure, making the GBM drift equal to the risk-free rate $r$ — the correct framework for option pricing and replication.

CALL PRICE — BLACK-SCHOLES
$$C = S \cdot N(d_1) - K e^{-rT} \cdot N(d_2)$$ $$d_1 = \frac{\ln(S/K) + (r + \sigma^2/2)\,T}{\sigma\sqrt{T}}, \quad d_2 = d_1 - \sigma\sqrt{T}$$
Put price follows from put-call parity: $P = C - S + Ke^{-rT}$.

THE GREEKS

The Greeks measure the sensitivity of the option price to each market parameter. Delta and gamma drive the hedging strategy; vega captures volatility risk.

DELTA Δ
$\Delta = N(d_1)$ — price change per €1 move in $S$. For a call, $\Delta \in [0, 1]$; for a put, $\Delta \in [-1, 0]$. The number of shares to hold in the hedge.
GAMMA Γ
$\Gamma = \frac{N'(d_1)}{S\sigma\sqrt{T}}$ — rate of change of delta. Peaks sharply at-the-money near expiry, where small price moves swing delta violently and hedging becomes hard.
VEGA ν
$\nu = S \cdot N'(d_1) \cdot \sqrt{T}$ — price change per 1% move in $\sigma$. Proportional to gamma: $\nu = S^2 \sigma T \cdot \Gamma$. A high-gamma position is also high-vega.

DELTA · GAMMA · VEGA — four maturities from T=1y to T=0.1y

MONTE CARLO SIMULATION

The simulation scales to 1,000,000 GBM paths, generated in chunks of 50,000 to cap memory usage under 200 MB regardless of path count. Each path is paired with its antithetic counterpart — the same random draw $Z$ and $-Z$ — which cancels noise algebraically and halves variance with no extra computation.

The hedging loop iterates over time steps, operating on all paths simultaneously as numpy arrays. This removes the outer path loop and gives a ~100× speedup over a naive nested loop.

DELTA HEDGING

P&L DISTRIBUTION — daily rebalancing, 1,000,000 paths

At each rebalancing step, the hedger adjusts the share position to $\Delta(t, S_t)$. With continuous rebalancing, the residual P&L is exactly zero. With discrete rebalancing, the tracking error scales as: $$\text{error} \propto \sigma \sqrt{\frac{T}{n}}$$ Halving the interval cuts the error by ~30%. The distribution should be centered on zero — a biased mean signals model miscalibration.

ERROR VS FREQUENCY — simulated error vs 1/√n theoretical

The empirical hedging error matches the theoretical 1/√n convergence law closely across all tested frequencies — from annual to daily rebalancing. This confirms the simulation is correctly calibrated and that the only path to tighter hedges, given fixed parameters, is to rebalance more often.

DELTA-GAMMA HEDGING

Delta hedging leaves gamma exposure: large moves in $S$ shift delta and break the hedge. Gamma hedging adds a second option to neutralize $\Gamma$ simultaneously:

GAMMA NEUTRALITY
$$h_2 = \frac{\Gamma_{\text{target}}}{\Gamma_{\text{hedge}}}, \quad h_1 = \Delta_{\text{target}} - h_2 \cdot \Delta_{\text{hedge}}$$
Two equations, two unknowns — $h_1$ shares and $h_2$ units of a second option with strike $K_{\text{hedge}} \neq K$.

With gamma neutralized, the portfolio survives larger moves between rebalancings. Error drops significantly at low rebalancing frequencies — the regime where gamma hedging pays off most.

P&L ATTRIBUTION

P&L ATTRIBUTION — gamma vs theta, mean over 1,000,000 paths

At each time step, the hedging P&L decomposes exactly into:

P&L DECOMPOSITION
$$\Delta\Pi = \underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\Gamma(\Delta S)^2}_{\text{gamma}} - \underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\Gamma\sigma^2 S^2 \, dt}_{\text{theta}}$$
These two terms cancel in expectation under Black-Scholes. The net P&L tracks the gap between realized and implied volatility.

When $\sigma_{\text{real}} > \sigma_{\text{hedge}}$, realized moves are larger than expected, gamma P&L dominates, and the hedger profits. When $\sigma_{\text{real}} < \sigma_{\text{hedge}}$: theta cost exceeds gamma gains, and P&L is negative. This is vega risk.

Sweeping $\sigma_{\text{hedge}}$ while keeping $\sigma_{\text{real}}$ fixed reveals a sharp crossover: the mean P&L is positive when the hedger underestimates vol (long gamma) and negative when they overestimate it (short gamma). The width of the distribution grows symmetrically away from the calibrated point — any vol mismatch costs money.

MEAN P&L VS σ_HEDGE — vol mismatch effect, 1,000,000 paths

IMPLIED VOLATILITY SURFACE

In practice, a single flat volatility $\sigma$ does not match observed market prices. The implied volatility surface $\sigma(K, T)$ maps each (strike, maturity) pair to its market-implied vol, built from live SPY option chains via yfinance.

The volatility skew is clearly visible — OTM puts (low strikes) carry significantly higher IV than OTM calls, reflecting the market's asymmetric fear of downside tail risk. The term structure shows short-dated vol spiking above the long-run level.

RBF + FAST GRID
The surface is fitted once via Radial Basis Function interpolation (thin-plate spline) on ~300 market quotes. A 200×100 precomputed grid then gives O(1) bilinear IV lookup during simulation — 100–1000× faster than direct RBF evaluation.

THEORY VS PRACTICE

THEORY VS PRACTICE — flat vol (BS) vs market surface, 1M paths

Running the same delta hedge under flat vol vs the real SPY surface quantifies exactly how much the volatility smile costs a delta hedger.

The flat-vol distribution (BS model) is tight and centred at zero — the hedge works when model matches reality. The market-surface distribution is dramatically wider: the hedger now faces skew and term structure that a constant-sigma delta cannot neutralise.
